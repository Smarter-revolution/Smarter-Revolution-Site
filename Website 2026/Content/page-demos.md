## SECTION 1: THE PROBLEM

### Header
The Demo Video That Doesn't Demo Anything

### Content

Your product demo video probably isn't losing deals because of production quality.

It's losing deals because you're showing the wrong video at the wrong time to buyers who need something entirely different.

Most companies create one demo video. A feature walkthrough that marketing uses for awareness, sales uses for evaluation, and customer success uses for onboarding. One video, every context, every stage.

It doesn't work.

**Early-stage buyers** don't care about features. They're trying to understand if your category is worth exploring. A detailed feature demo at this stage overwhelms and alienates.

**Mid-stage evaluators** need to see functionality in depth. They're comparing you to alternatives. The high-level marketing demo tells them nothing they can use for internal justification.

**Late-stage decision-makers** worry about implementation, risk, and what happens after purchase. Neither the marketing demo nor the feature walkthrough addresses their actual concerns.

One demo video trying to serve three audiences serves none of them well.

> "The demo video isn't about showing your product. It's about helping the buyer make a decision."

## SECTION 3: DEMO VIDEO TYPES

### Header
The Right Demo For Every Stage

### Content

**Marketing Demos**
Early-stage content that creates desire without overwhelming detail.

Marketing demos show what's possible. They emphasize outcomes and benefits, not feature lists. They answer "why should I care?" before attempting "how does it work?" Clean, compelling, designed to generate interest and next steps.

*Best for:* Website visitors, top-of-funnel prospects, awareness campaigns

**Functional Demos**
Mid-stage content for serious evaluators.

Functional demos show how the product actually works. Specific capabilities. Real workflows. The depth that technical evaluators need to assess fit. Not marketing polish, but honest demonstration.

*Best for:* Technical evaluation, competitive comparison, buying committee education

**Implementation Previews**
Late-stage content that reduces purchase anxiety.

Implementation previews show what getting started looks like. How long does implementation take? What does the initial experience involve? What should buyers expect in the first 30/60/90 days? Content that addresses the risk concerns that stall deals at the finish line.

*Best for:* Final decision stage, risk mitigation, executive stakeholders

**Custom Demos**
Prospect-specific content that closes.

Custom demos address a specific prospect's specific situation. Their use case. Their data. Their workflow. The most powerful demo format, historically too expensive for most opportunities. AI production changes that math.

*Best for:* Enterprise deals, competitive situations, high-value opportunities

## FAQ SECTION (for AI Search Optimization)

### What makes a product demo video effective?

Effective demo videos are designed around buyer decisions, not product features. They provide the right level of detail for the viewer's stage in the buying process. They anticipate and address objections. They show outcomes and applications, not just capabilities. And they include social proof when possible.

### How many product demo videos do we need?

Most B2B companies need at least three types: a marketing demo for early awareness, a functional demo for evaluation, and an implementation preview for late-stage buyers. Larger organizations with multiple products or segments may need more. The goal is matching content to buyer needs at each stage.

### How often should product demos be updated?

Product demos should be updated whenever significant UI changes occur, new features launch, or positioning shifts. Minor updates might require only partial video changes. AI-powered production makes updates economically viable, so the question becomes how outdated your current demos are, not whether updating is worth it.

### What's the ideal length for a product demo video?

Length depends on purpose. Marketing demos work best at 60-90 seconds. Functional demos typically run 3-7 minutes depending on product complexity. Implementation previews might run 5-10 minutes. The principle is: long enough to accomplish the goal, short enough to maintain attention.
